# Candidates — "The Best Meal You Ever Had"

Field note: the 111-submission field is saturated with grandmother's rendang/soto
and 2 a.m. warung lines, so all eight candidates below deliberately avoid the
grandmother's-kitchen shape and the midnight-warung trope. Every candidate is
one sentence, ≤ 200 characters, all words my own.

Criteria (judge order): (1) on-subject, (2) concrete, (3) true to one person's
memory, (4) one clean sentence, fresh wording, no cliche, (5) lands with an
image/turn/ending that stays. Each scored 1–5, total /25.

## Winner

**#8 (162 chars, 25/25)** — the submitted sentence.

## Per-criterion justifications — best 3

### #8 — Mango at the red light (25/25)

- **C1 (5):** Opens with "The best thing I ever ate" — the best-ever claim is explicit and the whole sentence is that one meal.
- **C2 (5):** Named food (mango), place (a red light, a bucket), texture (juice dripping), and one specific moment (eaten over the steering wheel).
- **C3 (5):** A transaction nobody else could report — a boy, a bucket, a traffic light — it could only be one person's memory, not a stock line.
- **C4 (5):** One clean sentence that reads aloud smoothly, no cliche, no borrowed phrasing; "selling them out of a bucket" is plain and fresh.
- **C5 (5):** "While the light stayed green" is the turn — the light that should have ended the moment holds it instead, a small charmed pause that stays after one reading.

### #3 — Burnt wings at the wedding (24/25)

- **C1 (5):** The wedding feast that failed and the wings that replaced it are framed as the best meal by the telling.
- **C2 (5):** Burnt chicken wings, a tray, behind the kitchen door — dish, place, texture and moment all named.
- **C3 (5):** A father-and-child theft at a family wedding is unrepeatable and specific.
- **C4 (5):** One clean sentence; "like thieves, laughing" is fresh and unforced.
- **C5 (4):** The ending image is warm and visual, but the turn is gentle rather than surprising — it lands, it does not twist.

### #6 — Banh mi in the rain (24/25)

- **C1 (4):** It never actually says this was the best meal — the claim is only implied by the reverent tone, so a strict judge could mark it down on the first criterion.
- **C2 (5):** Banh mi, one-handed fold, umbrella, rain on the crust — dish, place, texture and moment all present.
- **C3 (5):** A single vendor's small act of care; nobody else has this exact memory.
- **C4 (5):** Clean single sentence, fresh wording throughout, no cliche.
- **C5 (5):** "The rain made the crust taste like a small kindness" turns weather into flavor and ends on a line that stays.

## Why the others scored lower (honest notes)

- **#1 (23):** C1 4 — the best-ever claim is only implied ("never tasted anything so close to being found"); C4 4 — "a ferry that never docked" puzzles on first read.
- **#2 (22):** C2 4 — texture is thin (cold egg, warm bun is the whole sensory load); C5 4 — the ending is kind but the turn is mild.
- **#4 (23):** C1 4 — "no restaurant has ever caught up to it" implies best rather than stating it; C5 4 — the ending is a comparison, not an image.
- **#5 (23):** C4 4 — "more than any feast" leans on a stock contrast; C5 4 — the ending is tender but the phrasing is familiar.
- **#7 (22):** C3 4 — a home-cook memory many people could plausibly claim; C5 3 — it ends on the scene with no turn at all.
